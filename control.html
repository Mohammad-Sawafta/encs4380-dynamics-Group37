Curve and final-error tolerance: 10−6. For PI, tau s² + (1 + K Kp)s + K Ki gives wn = sqrt(K Ki / tau) and zeta = (1 + K Kp) / (2 sqrt(tau K Ki)). Zero step error requires a stable loop and a reachable target.

Equations, measurement rules, and limits

At every RK4 stage, e = r − y, I' = e, and u = clamp(Kp e + Ki I − Kd (y − z) / Tf). The derivative filter is z' = (y − z) / Tf with Tf = 0.02 s. When Ki or Kd is zero its unused state is frozen. All states start at zero.

The derivative acts on measured output, avoiding a kick from the setpoint step. The time step is limited using the plant and controller rates; plot sampling is separate from integration. This is a continuous-controller simulation, not P3's sampled Arduino controller. P1 has no anti-windup: a saturated integral controller can keep accumulating error.

Metrics use every integration step, independently of the fewer points drawn on the graphs. Shorten the duration to inspect very fast responses on the plots. Rise time uses the first 10% and 90% setpoint crossings, interpolated between integration steps. Overshoot is excess beyond the setpoint, normalized by its magnitude; negative steps use the direction of the step. Settling uses the final entry into the 2% target band and requires at least 5% of the simulation (or 0.1 s) inside afterward. Finite simulations cannot guarantee future settling.

A steady-state error is estimated only when the last 15% of output varies by at most 0.0001 × max(1, |r|). Otherwise the end-of-window error is shown separately. A zero step has no normalized timing or overshoot metrics. Increase the duration to inspect slow or oscillating responses.
